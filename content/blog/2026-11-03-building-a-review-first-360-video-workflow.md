I wanted editable Resolve timelines for a landscape video, vertical Shorts and Instagram versions of the same 360 footage. The footage came from an October 21 outing. I worked on the processing and validation described here on November 3.

### Test the stitching

I started with a four-second proof that included subject-tracking keyframes and separate output timelines. The footage used premium glass lens guards, but the initial calibration was for bare lenses. Applying the appropriate calibration substantially improved the stitching.

The next test was a 30-second review. Longer motion and difficult seams still needed inspection, along with tilt, recognition and final-resolution quality.

### Browse the source before rendering the edits

The review expanded to ten chronologically ordered clips, totaling about 70 minutes with audio. Lower-resolution copies made the material practical to browse in a local 360 player. I could identify a moment and viewing direction before building the edit.

Those choices could then become editable pan, tilt, zoom and transition keyframes. The review copies still had residual seam and tilt issues. The originals remained available, and the projects and review exports were verified on the Mac.

The result was a usable source-review workflow and editable timelines. Stitching quality still needed to be assessed for each shot before choosing it for final delivery.
