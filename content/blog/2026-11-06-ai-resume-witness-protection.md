I have more than twenty years of technology experience. Apparently, that was too much identifying information.

While helping me apply for jobs, an AI assistant produced a two-page résumé that removed my name, professional email, company, university and the names of the organizations where I had worked. It kept the degree. Somewhere in America, a Bachelor of Arts had happened.

My career had entered witness protection.

Instead of InXpress, the document offered “Global shipping and logistics platform.” SeeTickets became “Event-ticketing platform.” Intermountain became “Regional healthcare system.” Years of work had become a series of things I had done in unidentified buildings.

Then a rejection arrived, and I asked which résumé we had submitted. It was the anonymous one.

A hiring team was trying to decide whether I could do a specific job. We had removed the company names and professional identity that would help them evaluate my experience. We had given a busy reader more work.

Perhaps next time I could submit a blank PDF and let them enjoy the mystery.

### Privacy had swallowed the purpose

I want my home address, private phone number and credentials kept out of a public résumé. My professional name and the companies in my career history help a reader establish who performed the work.

The workflow had stretched a privacy instruction until it removed that evidence. It also imposed a two-page target on a career spanning healthcare, logistics, ticketing, finance, energy, mobile applications, backend systems, cloud infrastructure and developer tooling. I run Nokware, and consulting engagements often overlap.

Explaining that history requires judgment. A page limit is easier to enforce. The workflow reduced it to roughly six hundred words, and the document remained tidy.

My résumé had excellent margins. Its employment prospects were left to fend for themselves.

### Check the attachment that was submitted

When I compared rejection notices with the saved submissions, several applications had received anonymized versions. Others retained my identity but used heavily shortened histories. One rejection did not identify which of three applications it concerned. All three had received anonymized résumés.

The PDFs opened and looked professional. Reviews had passed, submissions had completed and confirmation pages existed. Those checks had missed a question I should have asked before the applications went out. Did the attachment still represent the applicant?

I recognize this failure in software. An API returns success or a file arrives, but the customer still cannot accomplish the task. Here, I needed a hiring team to understand how my experience applied to its role. We had checked that the attachment arrived and overlooked what we had removed from it.

### Put the evidence back

The résumé now starts from my complete career history. Tailoring emphasizes relevant work while keeping it attached to the engagement where it happened. My professional identity, company, employers and clients, university, dates and meaningful accomplishments remain available to the reader. Private contact details receive their own review.

Before submission, the exact final PDF gets checked for content and appearance. The attachment associated with the actual application gets verified too. A filename in an editor cannot establish which document the employer received.

I still use AI. But a request to make something concise now has to survive a review of the evidence it removed.

My name is Mish Ochu. I run Nokware. Both belong on my résumé.

The witness protection program was terrible for networking.
